_**Continuation of the Kamalari project in 2018**_

Child labour has been officially banned in Nepal since 2000. Even so, the exploitation of many girls in the south-west of the country, near the Indian border, is part of everyday life. They are sold as so-called "Kamalari – hard-working women" for a minimal wage of 40 to 50 euros a year. From as young as five, they have to toil as housemaids for wealthy families and landowners. Their working days are 14 to 16 hours long, and they are often defenceless against violence and sexual abuse.

Since shortly after our foundation was set up, we have been working to rescue Kamalari girls.

Among many ethnic groups, for example the Tharu, the deeply rooted traditions include the Kamaiya system, a form of bonded labour under which poor families sell their daughters to rich families as servants. There, the girls have to do hard physical work from childhood and usually have no chance of going to school. In the districts of Dang, Kailali and Kanchanpur, thousands of girls have already been freed from bondage. Many of them now live with their families again and go to school. However, only a few girls can continue their education after grade 10, because they have to earn their living. They therefore have hardly any opportunity to learn a trade or go to university. With our new project, we support 232 former Kamalari girls in continuing their education so that they can escape poverty in the long term. With the help of scholarships, they can finish secondary school, complete vocational training or study for a bachelor's degree. We help the girls set up study groups, advise them on different careers and help them prepare for job interviews.

During her stay of several months in Nepal, one of our daughters got to know many of these girls, went to lessons with them and lived with them for a few days in a "hostel" (a barrack with no proper floor, only mats on the ground, no stoves…).

**A book has now been published about the fate of one of these girls, whom we met personally in Nepal and who was enslaved for twelve years. Journalist Nathalie Schwaiger (whom we have also met since) wrote down the moving story of the former bonded labourer Urmila Chaudhary. "Sklavenkind. Verkauft, verschleppt, vergessen – Mein Kampf für Nepals Töchter" (Slave child. Sold, abducted, forgotten – my fight for Nepal's daughters) is available from [Droemer Knaur](http://www.droemer-knaur.de/buecher/Sklavenkind.7760860.html). Senta Berger wrote the foreword and recorded a fascinating audiobook. The book is also available in English: our daughter Annika translated it from German into English during her stay in Nepal.**
