**_Building the first school for women in Nepal – background and challenges:_**

Makwanpur is one of Nepal's 75 districts and borders the capital, Kathmandu. Around 392,604 people live in the district's 43 villages. 90 percent of the population depend on agriculture, yet only about 40 percent of households can provide for their own needs. Education is inadequate, especially in rural areas: on average, one teacher looks after 60 pupils. Enrolment rates are rising significantly, but so is the need for staff and space. Most school buildings are in poor condition. The rooms are too small and too dark, and there is a lack of teachers and suitable teaching and learning materials. Schools also often have too little drinking water and no separate toilets for girls and boys.

**The situation:**

Education is particularly difficult for women. Early marriage, childbirth and poverty have kept many women from going to school, and there is great inequality between men and women in education in Nepal. At the Shree Mahendra secondary school, these women have the chance – for the first time in Nepal – to make up for the schooling they missed. They are taught by volunteer teachers, from grade 2 to grade 8. How important these lessons are is shown by the steadily rising number of participants: the project started in mid-June 2007 with 105 women, and now more than 460 women aged between 20 and 60 attend. As the school's classrooms are more than full during the day, the women's lessons have to take place before regular school hours, from six to ten in the morning. Despite these difficult conditions, the women's motivation is unbroken. Many walk two hours every day, even though they have a family with children to look after at home.

**Goals:**

The main goal is to give the women of the region a better learning environment by building new rooms, so that their lessons can take place during regular school hours. The aim is a significant increase in the literacy rate. The new educational opportunity also strengthens the role of women. The pupils of Shree Mahendra benefit from the new rooms as well.

**Measures:**

To create a better learning environment, the following measures are being carried out:

- Building a new school building (with six rooms, each about 4.9 x 6.7 m)
- Providing school furniture for the classrooms
- Planting the grounds to create a pleasant atmosphere

A group of nine teachers, community members and parents will coordinate the work. They are responsible for the success of the construction and all other work involved. Plan supports the project above all with technical know-how and is responsible for its quality assurance.
