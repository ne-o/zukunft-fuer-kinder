## The situation in Nepal

![](images/archiv/1389816231.webp)

Since 1996, the country has been shaken by domestic political unrest. For a long time, violent clashes, strikes, kidnappings and curfews dominated everyday life in Nepal. The situation of children is marked by violations of their rights. Early marriage and motherhood of girls, trafficking in girls, child labour and even child prostitution, with a high risk of HIV infection, are still widespread.

The health system is underdeveloped, with high maternal and child mortality. More than half of the population is chronically undernourished. Contaminated drinking water, a lack of latrines and poor hygiene add to this. Education and vocational training are also in great need of development.

The illiteracy rate among women in rural areas is very high. There are too few schools, and the way to school is long. Parents depend on their children's help, above all with work in the fields.

Nepal is one of the poorest countries in the world, and people there live in conditions that are unimaginable for us. Many families live in the simplest houses without electricity. A quarter of the population lives in absolute poverty. There are hardly any jobs. Most people work in agriculture. Many live in debt bondage.

In this environment, the Schönebeck Stiftung _„Zukunft für Kinder“_ wants to support Plan's work in child-centred community development.
