**_Emergency aid for Pakistan after the devastating floods_**

In Punjab province, the river levels are falling. But many children and adults have skin and respiratory diseases, diarrhoea or other infections such as cholera, and there are many snake bites. In Sindh province, the water has risen again. Many people had to leave their homes and flee to higher ground. Near the city of Thatta (100 km south-east of the port city of Karachi) in Sindh province, a dam burst on Friday morning. Pakistani TV channels reported that more than 175,000 people had fled the city and were seeking shelter in surrounding towns and villages. Plan does not work in the city of Thatta.

The Schönebeck-Stiftung „Zukunft für Kinder“ supports Plan International in providing emergency aid in the districts of Layyah, Muzaffargarh and Rajanpur in Punjab province and in the districts of Ghotki, Khairpur and Thatta in Sindh province. The teams work both in the shelters set up by the government and in camps set up spontaneously on higher ground. To help the many people still holding out in the flooded areas, Plan staff also accompany army helicopter missions and distribute relief supplies in Plan's six areas of operation in Punjab and Sindh. Plan handed out cooked meals in the camps but is now distributing more and more dry food. 70 percent of the refugees in the emergency shelters are mothers and children, including countless breastfeeding women. They can prepare the dry food themselves as needed.

Ramadan began on 11 August. The Islamic month of fasting is as important in Pakistan as the Christmas season is in Western countries. This year, the end of Ramadan is expected to fall on 10/11 September; the exact date also depends on the sighting of the new moon and may only be set the evening before. Schools in Pakistan officially started on 14 August, Independence Day. According to the UN, however, 1.6 million children cannot go to school at the moment because their schools have been destroyed or are being used as emergency shelters.
