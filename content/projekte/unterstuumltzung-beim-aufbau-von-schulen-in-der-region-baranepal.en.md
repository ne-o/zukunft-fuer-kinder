title: Helping to build schools in the Bara region, Nepal
---

**_Helping to build schools in the Bara region, Nepal – the situation_**

In the Bara region, agriculture is the main source of income – unfortunately not a very productive one. Seasonal unemployment is also widespread. There is hardly any local industry, so the population additionally depends on imports from abroad. Many young people emigrate to India to find work.

**The challenge**

The illiteracy rate is very high, especially in rural areas and among women. Primary school is free, but many children cannot attend because there are no schools or the way to school is too long. Many children also have to help their parents with their work. In recent years, enrolment rates in Nepal have risen sharply: through various media such as radio and community meetings, people in Nepal have become more aware of the importance of education. As a result, alternative forms of education are also becoming more popular – early childhood care and adult education, for example, are increasingly in demand. This in turn increases the need for space, well-trained teachers and learning materials. The result is overcrowded classrooms. Schools also rarely have child-friendly toilets, and girls in particular feel unsafe without separate facilities. In short, the environment is not good enough for pupils to learn properly. The entire school infrastructure needs to be expanded.

**Goal**

The main goal of the project is to increase enrolment rates in the Bara region. Primary school enrolment is to rise from 74 to 90 percent. In future, 60 percent of children rather than 31 percent are to complete a lower secondary school. In addition, the quality of teaching is to improve and the gender balance is to become more even.

**Measures**

To improve the learning environment, the following will be done in a total of five communities:

- five schools renovated and completed
- classrooms equipped with school furniture (216 desks and benches)
- latrines built for girls and boys

**Measures carried out so far**

Since the start of the project, the following measures have been carried out:

- assessment of the schools whose buildings are still unfinished
- agreements made with community members
- construction work at three schools (Sri Mahavir primary school, Sri Nepal Rastriya primary school Utterjhitkaiya, Sri Nepal Rastriya primary school Bhaluibharbaliya)

**Beneficiaries**

The 1,446 girls and 1,233 boys at the five state schools in Gadahal, Banjariya, Ineruwasira, Dohari and Dahiyar in the Bara region benefit directly from the project.

**What beneficiaries say**

**Duration and budget**

The project activities began in January 2009 and are expected to continue until the end of the year.
By the end of March 2009, around 28,148 US dollars had gone into the project activities.
