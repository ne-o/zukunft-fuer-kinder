title: Emergency aid for the East Africa drought

**_Disaster relief in East Africa – the current situation_**

The drought in East Africa is getting worse, and more and more malnourished, weakened children are falling ill with infections such as diarrhoea or measles. According to United Nations estimates, 2.5 billion US dollars are needed to help the 13.3 million people going hungry in the region. The drought is expected to last until March 2012, as the next rainy season – and with it the harvests – is also likely to fail.
Our partner Plan has been working in Ethiopia and Kenya for many years; South Sudan is the children's charity's newest partner country. In all three countries, Plan has close contacts with local organisations and authorities, which makes relief operations much easier.

**Help for the people of Ethiopia**
In Ethiopia, the centre and the south of the country are hit hardest by the drought. In Oromia, the largest and most populous state, it normally rains from March to May and from October to December. The first rainy season usually brings 70 percent of the annual rainfall, which is especially important for farming. The region is actually "Ethiopia's breadbasket", where wheat and barley are harvested for large parts of the country. But now two important rainy seasons have failed, and all stocks of grain and beans are used up. Rising prices and conflicts make the crisis in Ethiopia worse: in the last two years, the price of maize has tripled, and armed conflicts over land use keep breaking out between the many ethnic groups. Around 4.6 million Ethiopians are going hungry and depend on humanitarian aid. Plan provides emergency aid in the states of Amhara, Oromia and "Southern Nations, Nationalities and Peoples" (SNNPR) and in Addis Ababa, helping more than 500,000 people on the ground. Volunteers and partner organisations are currently distributing food rations – maize, beans and oil – to families, as well as a protein-rich mix of cereals and soya.

**Water and food in Kenya**
In northern and north-eastern Kenya, around 3.75 million people depend on food aid. Plan will support 460,000 Kenyans over the next six months; 295,000 children and mothers are already receiving food. Schools, health centres and other public facilities in the programme areas of Tharaka, Kwale, Kilifi and Machakos and in Kitui South have been supplied with water. Children, pregnant women and breastfeeding mothers have also received vitamin-rich supplementary food. In addition, wells are being repaired, school meals provided and child protection training held.

**Emergency aid in South Sudan**
In South Sudan, Plan is supporting 280,000 people affected by the drought. In addition to its help in the Juba Lainya programme area, the team supports children in Central Equatoria and communities in the states of Eastern Equatoria and Jonglei. The recent fighting in the region has not hindered Plan's aid so far. However, heavy rain in almost all parts of the country has destroyed many roads and is currently preventing school feeding and food transport in general. Plan's work to improve sanitation can also only continue once the floods are over. Experts expect South Sudan's grain harvest to fall by at least 40 percent this year.
