---
title: Scholarship project in Ghana
---

**_Scholarship programmes for girls in Ghana – background:_**

Very few children in Ghana receive secondary education. In developing the education system, the government's focus is on primary education, which is free, and the books needed are provided as well. Until now, there has been no government support for secondary education. The rising costs of food, accommodation, books and stationery make it impossible for many families to pay for schooling. As a result, fewer than 45 percent of pupils attend secondary school. The situation is particularly difficult in rural areas. Girls are especially disadvantaged, because in the face of financial worries many parents fall back on traditional roles and favour their sons.

**Aim of the project:**

The aim of the project is to give 325 girls in rural parts of the country access to further education. 200 girls receive a four-year scholarship for a secondary school, 25 girls are supported through a four-year university degree, and another 100 girls complete three years of vocational training.

**Measures and results:** the scholarships for the first three semesters have been paid. At Christmas 2008, a holiday camp was held for 40 scholarship holders from the Central Region, to encourage the girls to get to know each other and share experiences. Besides discussions, the programme included workshops on topics such as children's rights.

The project makes an important contribution to widening the focus of the government's efforts to include secondary education and to bringing about lasting change. The government has already announced that it will take such measures and promote secondary education as a means of tackling youth unemployment.
The girls at this school are particularly successful learners – so far, not a single one of them has dropped out.

**Participation and beneficiaries:**

The families contribute to the project's success, for example by paying for their daughters' fares, school uniforms, books and stationery. The project benefits not only the 325 scholarship holders but also their families and communities. The girls are seen as role models in their communities, and many younger girls look up to them and their achievements. In this way, education and training for girls are increasingly becoming a topic in public and in the communities.
